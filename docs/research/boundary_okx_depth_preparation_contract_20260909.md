# Counted OKX depth: prospective observation contract

The next predictive hypothesis is that price-level order counts add information beyond price and displayed quantity. Aggregate quantity can be identical while the number of resting orders differs. The mean quantity per order is Q/N; neither individual order sizes nor cancellations versus executions are identified by an absolute depth update.

The initial preparation covers the already decoded BTC June 1 source as a full-day engineering preflight. A later, separately frozen preparation will cover the 28 acquired BTC/ETH sources from May 29 through June 11, 2023. No fresh June 13–July 2 confirmation data enters either preparation. Source preparation reads only the original Binance `decision_time` column and creates no target or model score.

For each original decision d, and each registered assumed delay L in {100, 500} milliseconds, define c = 100 floor((d-L)/100). Only messages with publisher timestamp strictly less than c enter the sampled state. All messages at c are excluded. The archive does not expose local arrival clocks. Sampling the archive at these boundaries is an information restriction; it does not prove equivalence to a particular live OKX feed.

Book reconstruction preserves original decimal price keys, absolute decimal quantities and integer counts. Bid and ask changes from one message are applied atomically. A full 400-level snapshot initializes the complete known price range. After updates introduce more than 400 known positive levels on a side, the worst levels are removed and that side's known frontier moves inward. It never expands until a new full snapshot. An isolated update outside the frontier cannot certify the missing range between it and the known book. A top-k observation is available only if at least k known positive levels remain on each side.

A publisher gap exceeding 1,000ms, an empty known side, or a crossed book invalidates the state until the next full snapshot. At a query, a book older than 1,000ms relative to c is unavailable even if the next message has not yet arrived. Each recovery starts a new coverage segment. Ordinary full snapshots during uninterrupted coverage do not reset the segment. History features will require valid observations throughout their lag window, exact clock separation, and the same coverage segment.

The sidecar stores up to 100 ordered levels per side: float64 prices and quantities, int64 order counts, source timestamps, query cutoffs, segment IDs and known-depth counts. Floating-point projection must preserve positive quantities and strictly ordered, uncrossed prices. Unavailable observations are zero padded with explicit missing-clock and known-depth masks. All original decision rows remain in place; later model comparisons may not drop difficult rows according to OKX availability.

The archive reader checks its registered SHA256, UTC date, instrument, one correctly named tar member, bounded decompressed size and record length, and full gzip CRC/length trailer. No archive is extracted to the filesystem. The maximum stored grid is 90,000 decisions, two delays and 100 levels; arrays are limited to 1GiB per source. The preflight uses one worker and a 1,800-second wall limit. The complete preparation may use two data workers under a separate fixed protocol. All attempts and failures are retained.

The future model test must include quantity-only and quantity-plus-count features from the same OKX stream, at matched depth, delay, training/validation dates, existing Binance features and targets. Cross-exchange raw quantities cannot be divided without a unit conversion: OKX derivative quantities represent contracts. Within-book normalized quantities, count imbalance and relative mean-order-size ratios avoid that mismatch. Counts do not establish trader identities or market-order intent.

This coverage certificate is conditional on the publisher reporting all changes inside its advertised window. The archive lacks sequence IDs, so gaps below the threshold and undetected loss cannot be ruled out. Missing masks and assumed delays are retained as model inputs and evaluation conditions, not filled with imagined observations.

[OKX's live documentation](https://tr.okx.com/docs-v5/en/#order-book-trading-market-data-ws-order-book-channel) describes 400-level `books` updates at 100ms and restricts the 10ms `books-l2-tbt` channel to VIP4 and above. Free historical access does not imply free access to that live channel. The current live four-field level schema also differs from the observed historical three-field schema; the historical parser is based on the acquired source records.

[Gould and Bonart's queue-imbalance study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03492) motivates testing queue composition, but it studies next-price direction in Nasdaq stocks. Its reported improvements do not establish a five-second crypto prediction gain or the incremental value of order counts here.
